# How to Save Money in Berlin as an International Student (2026 Guide)

*Last updated: October 2026 · By the Settleezy Team · Reading time: 9 minutes*

**Quick answer:** Most international students in Berlin need about **€1,100–€1,350 a month** in 2026. The biggest savings come from three places: **rent** (a student dorm costs about half of an average €659 shared-flat room), **transport** (use the Deutschland-Semesterticket at €37.80/month instead of paying for single tickets), and **food** (cook at home, eat at the university Mensa, and shop at discount supermarkets). Phone plans, free culture and second-hand furniture can save another €50–€150 a month.

## 1. Save money on rent in Berlin

Rent is the biggest cost for students in Berlin. According to the MLP Student Housing Report 2026, an average 20 m² room in a shared flat (WG) in Berlin costs **€659 per month**, making Berlin the second most expensive German university city after Munich.

1. **Apply for a Studierendenwerk Berlin dorm as early as possible.** Dorm rooms are usually far cheaper than the private market, but waiting lists can be months long. Apply as soon as you have your admission letter.
2. **Look in outer districts.** Rooms in Lichtenberg, Marzahn-Hellersdorf, Spandau, Reinickendorf, Wedding and Neukölln's southern parts are often cheaper than Mitte, Prenzlauer Berg or Kreuzberg. With a semester ticket, the commute costs nothing extra.
3. **Choose a WG over a studio.** Rent, internet and the €18.36/month broadcasting fee (Rundfunkbeitrag) are charged per flat, so sharing splits these costs.
4. **Never pay before you have seen the room and signed a contract.** Housing scams are common in Berlin and target new international students. See our guide to [student housing in Berlin without scams](/guides/student-housing-berlin/).
5. **Check the deposit.** By law (§ 551 BGB) a deposit can be at most three months of cold rent, and you may pay it in three instalments.

## 2. Save money on transport in Berlin

6. **Use your Deutschland-Semesterticket.** Most public universities in Berlin include it in the semester fee. From winter semester 2026/27 it costs **€226.80 per semester (€37.80/month)** and covers all local and regional transport across Germany.
7. **No semester ticket? Get the Deutschlandticket.** Many private universities don't include a semester ticket. The regular Deutschlandticket costs **€63/month** in 2026. That is still much cheaper than buying single tickets.
8. **Always validate your ticket.** Riding without a valid ticket costs a €60 fine.
9. **Buy a used bike.** Second-hand bikes on Kleinanzeigen or at flea markets cost €80–€200 and pay for themselves fast.
10. **Travel Germany on your ticket.** The Deutschland(semester)ticket covers regional trains (RE, RB, S-Bahn), so weekend trips to Potsdam, Leipzig or the Baltic Sea cost nothing extra.

More detail: [Public transport and the semester ticket in Berlin](/guides/public-transport-berlin-students/).

## 3. Save money on food in Berlin

11. **Eat at the Mensa.** Studierendenwerk Berlin's university canteens sell student meals for a few euros. In 2025 the daily dish cost €1.75.
12. **Shop at discount supermarkets.** Aldi, Lidl, Netto and Penny are much cheaper than Rewe or Edeka for the same basics.
13. **Return your bottles (Pfand).** Most plastic bottles and cans carry a €0.25 deposit, which you get back at the machine in any supermarket.
14. **Use Too Good To Go and foodsharing.** Bakeries and restaurants sell surplus food at about a third of the price. Foodsharing "Fairteiler" fridges give food away for free.
15. **Cook in batches with flatmates.** Splitting bulk rice, lentils, oil and spices cuts costs for everyone.

## 5. Save money on insurance, banking and fees

18. **Choose public health insurance if you're eligible.** Students under 30 at a state-recognised university can join public insurance at the student rate. In 2026 that is about **€146/month** at TK for students aged 23+ without children. Think carefully before opting out: an exemption usually can't be reversed for the rest of your studies. See [health insurance for international students](/guides/health-insurance-students-germany/).
19. **Use a free bank account.** Several German and online banks offer free current accounts for students. Avoid accounts with monthly fees.
20. **Compare blocked account (Sperrkonto) providers.** In 2026 the visa requirement is **€11,904 per year (€992/month)**, and providers charge different setup and monthly fees.

## 6. Save money on furniture and everyday items

21. **Buy second-hand.** Kleinanzeigen, Facebook Marketplace and Berlin flea markets (such as Mauerpark on Sundays) are full of cheap furniture and kitchen items.
22. **Look for "zu verschenken" (free to give away).** Berliners often leave usable items on the street or post them for free online.

## 7. Free and cheap fun in Berlin

23. **Get student discounts everywhere.** Show your student ID for reduced museum, cinema, theatre and zoo tickets. Spotify, Apple Music, Amazon Prime and many software tools have student prices.
24. **Use the ClassicCard if you're under 30.** For €28 a year you get opera and ballet tickets for €18, and concerts for €16, at 10 Berlin venues.
25. **Enjoy free Berlin.** Tempelhofer Feld, the East Side Gallery, the Berlin Wall Memorial, the Reichstag dome (free with registration) and tip-based walking tours cost little or nothing.

More ideas: [Free and cheap things to do in Berlin for students](/guides/free-things-to-do-berlin-students/).

## 8. Earn money while you study

Non-EU students can work **140 full days or 280 half days per year** without extra permission. Germany's minimum wage is **€13.90/hour** in 2026, and a minijob lets you earn up to **€603/month** with no tax and no social contributions. See [student jobs in Berlin](/guides/student-jobs-berlin/).

## FAQ

### How much money does an international student need per month in Berlin?
In 2026, plan for about €1,100–€1,350 per month if you rent a room in a shared flat, or about €850–€950 in a student dorm. Rent (average €659 for a WG room), health insurance (about €146) and food (€200–€300) are the largest costs.

### Is €992 a month enough to live in Berlin as a student?
It can be enough if you live in a student dorm or a room under about €450, cook at home and use your semester ticket. With an average WG room at €659, €992 usually isn't enough without a part-time job or savings.

### What is the cheapest way to get around Berlin as a student?
The Deutschland-Semesterticket, included in most public universities' semester fee, costs €37.80/month from winter semester 2026/27 and covers all local transport in Germany. Without it, the Deutschlandticket at €63/month is the cheapest option for regular travel.

### Where do students eat cheaply in Berlin?
University canteens (Mensa) run by Studierendenwerk Berlin are the cheapest option, with meals for a few euros. Discount supermarkets (Aldi, Lidl, Netto, Penny), Too Good To Go and foodsharing points also help.
