# Health Insurance for International Students in Germany (2026): Public vs Private

*Last updated: October 2026 · By the Settleezy Team*

**Quick answer:** Health insurance is **mandatory** to enrol at a German university. Most international students **under 30** at a state-recognised university join **public (statutory) health insurance** at the student rate, which costs about **€140–€150 per month** in 2026 (TK: €146.29 for students 23+ without children). Students over 30, language-course students and some others usually need **private** insurance. If you opt out of public insurance, the exemption normally **can't be reversed** for the rest of your studies.

## Public vs private health insurance for students

| | Public (gesetzlich) | Private |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly cost 2026 | ≈ €140–€150 (student rate) | From about €40–€150, depending on the plan |
| Who can join | Students up to 30 (with some exceptions) at state-recognised universities | Anyone, but plans vary widely |
| Coverage | Full, set by law; no exclusions for pre-existing conditions | Depends on contract; may exclude pre-existing conditions, dental or mental health |
| Switching back | — | Exemption from public insurance is binding for your entire studies |
| Accepted for enrolment | Yes | Only if your university and the public insurer accept the exemption |

## What does public student insurance cost in 2026?

The student rate has two parts:
- **Health insurance:** €87.38/month base + the insurer's additional contribution (Zusatzbeitrag). At TK that's €110.38 in 2026.
- **Long-term care insurance (Pflegeversicherung):** €35.91/month if you're 23 or older with no children, €30.78 if you're under 23.

**Total at TK in 2026: €146.29/month** (23+, no children). Other public insurers such as AOK, DAK and Barmer charge similar amounts, varying with their additional contribution.

## Which insurance should I choose?

1. **Under 30 and starting a degree at a public or state-recognised university?** Public insurance is usually the safest choice.
2. **Over 30, doing a language course or Studienkolleg?** You usually need private or incoming-student insurance.
3. **Thinking of private insurance to save money?** Read the exclusions. Once you're exempt from public insurance, you can't normally switch back while you're a student.

## How to get insured

1. Choose a public insurer (e.g. TK, AOK, DAK, Barmer) and apply online.
2. The insurer sends your **insurance confirmation electronically** to your university.
3. Set up monthly payments from your German bank account.
4. You'll receive your **health insurance card (eGK)** by post.

## FAQ

### How much is health insurance for international students in Germany?
Public student health insurance costs about €140–€150 per month in 2026. At TK it's €146.29 for students aged 23+ without children.

### Is health insurance mandatory for students in Germany?
Yes. You need proof of health insurance to enrol at a German university and for your residence permit.

### Can I switch from private to public health insurance as a student?
Usually not. If you get an exemption from public insurance at the start of your studies, it normally applies for your entire studies.

### Do students over 30 get public health insurance?
Usually not at the student rate. Students over 30 generally need voluntary public insurance at a higher rate, or private insurance.
