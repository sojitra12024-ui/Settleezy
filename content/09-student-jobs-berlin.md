# Student Jobs in Berlin for International Students: Rules, Pay & Taxes (2026)

*Last updated: October 2026 · By the Settleezy Team*

**Quick answer:** Non-EU students in Germany can work **140 full days or 280 half days per year** without extra permission. During lecture periods, keep to about **20 hours per week** so you keep your student status. The minimum wage is **€13.90/hour** in 2026. A **minijob** lets you earn up to **€603/month** without tax or social security deductions. EU students can work like German students.

## Work rules for non-EU students (2026)

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Annual limit | 140 full days or 280 half days per year |
| Full day vs half day | More than 4 hours = full day; up to 4 hours = half day |
| During lecture periods | Up to about 20 hours/week to keep student insurance and status |
| Semester breaks | Full-time work is allowed (counts toward the annual limit) |
| Student assistant jobs at the university (HiWi) | Generally don't count toward the limit; check with the Ausländerbehörde |
| Mandatory internships | Don't count toward the limit |
| Self-employment / freelancing | Needs permission from the immigration office |

*Breaking these rules can put your residence permit at risk. If in doubt, ask your university's international office or the Landesamt für Einwanderung (LEA).*

## Types of student jobs and what they pay

| Job type | Pay / limit | Deductions |
|---|---|---|
| Minijob | Up to €603/month | No income tax, no social contributions (except a small optional pension contribution) |
| Werkstudent (working student) | Often €15–€20+/hour, up to ~20 h/week in term | Pension insurance only; income tax if above the allowance |
| HiWi (university assistant) | Set by the university, often above minimum wage | Depends on contract |
| Hospitality, delivery, retail | At least €13.90/hour | Depends on minijob or regular contract |

## What you need before you start

1. **Anmeldung** → you get your **tax ID (Steuer-ID)** by post. [How to do Anmeldung](/guides/anmeldung-berlin-students/)
2. **German bank account** for your salary
3. **Health insurance** confirmation
4. **Social security number**, issued when you start your first job
5. Check your **residence permit** for any work conditions

## Where to find student jobs in Berlin

- Your university's job portal and career service
- **Studierendenwerk Berlin's student job agency** ("Heinzelmännchen")
- Job sites: StepStone, Indeed, LinkedIn, Werkstudent-specific portals
- English-friendly startups and companies (Berlin has many)
- Notice boards at university and in your network

## FAQ

### How many hours can international students work in Germany?
Non-EU students can work 140 full days or 280 half days per year. During lecture periods, students usually stay at or below 20 hours per week.

### What is the minimum wage for students in Berlin in 2026?
€13.90 per hour (general statutory minimum wage). Some internships are exempt.

### How much can I earn in a minijob in 2026?
Up to €603 per month without income tax or social security contributions.

### Do I pay tax as a student in Germany?
Only if your yearly income goes above the basic tax-free allowance. Tax may be withheld from your pay but refunded when you file a tax return.
